# FORGE Evaluation Methodology

| Dimension | High | Medium | Low |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stakeholder value | Directly addresses a documented priority with a measurable stakeholder outcome. | Plausible indirect benefit; affected users or outcome still need validation. | Weak connection to the stated problem or documented stakeholder need. |
| Business value | Relevant measured economic evidence plus a credible local value mechanism. | Plausible cost/revenue mechanism; local baseline and economics unvalidated. | No credible economic mechanism or costs likely dominate the proposed benefit. |
| Technical feasibility | Relevant real-world deployments or strong pilots demonstrate the application. | Working prototype or adjacent deployment; substantial integration remains. | Primarily theoretical or laboratory evidence for this application. |
| Technology maturity | Repeated commercial deployment for this application. | Demonstrated prototype, pilot, or early commercialization. | Academic research or laboratory prototype. |
| Implementation difficulty | Major integration, qualification, capital, or workflow changes needed. | Bounded integration and training with known dependencies. | Routine configuration with existing capabilities and minimal workflow change. |
| Differentiation | Evidence supports a defensible capability, exclusive asset, or hard-to-copy advantage. | Potential temporary advantage; imitability or competitor adoption unclear. | Widely available capability mainly supports parity or operational improvement. |
| Evidence strength | Multiple independent, directly relevant empirical sources support the application. | One directly relevant empirical source or several adjacent primary sources. | Concepts, vendor claims, simulations, or weakly transferable evidence only. |
| Human impact | Substantial changes to authority, workload, skills, or livelihoods. | New tasks or training; existing accountability can be retained. | Minor workflow changes with little effect on roles or decision authority. |
| Risk | Material unresolved safety, privacy, regulatory, or business failure exposure. | Material risks with plausible controls requiring validation. | Limited, reversible exposure with established controls. |
| Time to implementation | Estimated over 3 years to scoped deployment; major dependencies remain. | Estimated 1–3 years to scoped deployment with dependencies. | Estimated under 1 year to scoped deployment with existing capabilities. |

Unknown: approved evidence is insufficient to select an anchor. State what evidence is missing.

High is not always desirable: difficulty, human impact, risk and time describe burden or exposure. No overall numerical score is calculated. Ratings are model judgments against anchors, not independently verified measurements.

## Evidence strength

- **High:** Direct primary empirical evidence relevant to the precise finding; method and limitations disclosed.
- **Medium:** Credible primary description or adjacent empirical result; transfer or outcome unverified.
- **Low:** Marketing claim, concept, indirect source, or poorly supported inference.

## Interpretation

Ratings apply to the proposed application, not the technology in the abstract. Maturity, feasibility and evidence strength are different questions. A real source may support a narrow factual finding while offering weak support for a local business intervention. Time estimates are planning judgments, not predictions. No overall weighted score is calculated.

The demo rubric judgments are curated examples. Live judgments are generated against the same anchors, then checked for complete dimensions and approved citation IDs. Semantic fit to anchors still needs human review.
